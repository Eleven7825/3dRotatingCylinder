\section{Computational setup}

Figure~\ref{fig:domain} shows the computational domain and the initial
configuration of the cylinder at $t = 0$. The cylinder is a rigid circular
body with its axis aligned with $z$ and its center of mass released at the
origin, free to translate under a prescribed oscillatory cross-flow forcing
and an effective (reduced-buoyancy) gravity $g_{\mathrm{eff}}$ acting in
$-y$. Dimensions for all settings are given in Table~\ref{tab:dims}.

Two different $z$-boundary settings are used across the case suite. In the
\emph{periodic} setting (panel a), the cylinder spans the full domain in $z$
and the $z$-boundaries wrap around, so the body is effectively infinite in
the spanwise sense. In the \emph{wall} setting (panel b), the domain is
closed off by solid walls and the cylinder is a finite length, centered in
$z$ with a gap to each wall.

The fin-series cases (panels c, d) use the periodic setting, with one or two
thin circular fins mounted perpendicular to the cylinder axis at evenly
spaced $z$-locations.

\subsection{Nondimensional parameters}

Following the rotational and gravitational Reynolds numbers and density-ratio
parameter used in the companion 2D curvilinear study
(\href{https://github.com/Eleven7825/curvlinear/blob/master/results/presentations/main.pdf}{\texttt{curvlinear/results/presentations/main.pdf}}),
\begin{equation}
    Re_r = \frac{\rho_f \, \Omega \, R^2}{\mu}, \qquad
    Re_g = \frac{\sqrt{\pi \rho_f^2 \, g \, \Delta \, R^3}}{\mu}, \qquad
    \Delta = \frac{\rho_s - \rho_f}{\rho_f},
\end{equation}
evaluated with the dimensional parameters in Table~\ref{tab:nondim-inputs}.
In this code, $\rho_s = \rho_f$ exactly, and gravity is applied as
$-\delta g$ rather than $-g$, with $\delta = 0.011$ for every case in the
suite. This buoyancy multiplier $\delta$ plays the role of $\Delta$
directly: the net body force $\rho_f \delta g V$ is equivalent to a real
density mismatch $\Delta = \delta$ acting under $g$.
The fin-series and baseline cases prescribe no net spin; the cylinder
instead oscillates translationally as $U_\infty \cos(2\pi f t)$. $Re_r$ is
evaluated using the angular frequency of that prescribed oscillation,
$\Omega = 2\pi f$, as the characteristic unsteady rate in place of a
physical spin.

All dimensional quantities in the code (and below) are in CGS units.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Trajectories}

Figure~\ref{fig:traj-abcd} shows the center-of-mass trajectories of cases
(a)--(d), each run in the domain of Figure~\ref{fig:domain} and plotted
against that same domain (red dashed box).

Case (b) (wall $z$, no fins) glides on an essentially straight, steep path
and exits the domain's lower boundary well before the others. Cases (a),
(c), and (d) (all periodic $z$) instead curve over and flatten out, leveling
off around $y \approx -30$ to $-40$ as $x$ approaches the domain's right
boundary -- their final points all land in a similar band, with the
one-fin and two-fin cases (c, d) tracking close to the finless case (a)
rather than diverging from it.
